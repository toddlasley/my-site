I find my reaction to the news of Microsoft shuttering many jobs at Xbox (more specifically Halo Studios) to be more plaintive than the news itself: I'm unsurprised and indifferent. As a devoted fan of Halo for what has been most of life now, I think I've reached a point where I refuse to devote any more emotional energy into the franchise or the monolithic mega corp that owns it. While I obviously have sympathy for anyone impacted by these layoffs, many many years of 343-I mean-Halo Studios fumbling the franchise has me feeling that this is long overdue. It seems evident that Halo: Campaign Evolved would not serve to be the spark needed to keep the studio alive, which is indicative of how well leadership had their fingers on the pulse of the community: no one asked for a second remake of a 25 year-old game. Despite my skepticism for the demand around Campaign Evolved, the decision to remake the game was originally framed as an excuse for the studio to experiment with Unreal Engine, as opposed to building games with an original engine like they did with Infinite. I guess I thought that this ticket to experimentation meant that they had more time. Maybe I assumed they had unlimited time, given how long they had been struggling to deliver prior to this.

They were so close with Infinite. The competitive scene was as healthy as it had been in a while, even though the game struggled to maintain a significant casual audience due to a premature launch and a general lack of content over the course of the game's service. Can Activision do better? It's hard for me to be optimistic. Other than managing to sell a shit ton of copies, I'm not sure that Call of Duty fans really enjoy their games. And while I echo the sentiment that many share in that Activision can hardly do worse than what 343/Halo Studios has, it's also hard for me to get excited about a singular publisher owning the keys to two of the biggest multiplayer first-person shooters in gaming.

I'm calling this a requiem, but it's hard to acknowledge the death of something that was likely already dead in the first place. I'm sure I will fall victim to the optimism that I have for years and decide to start paying attention when the next game gets closer to launch, but we all know that is a few years from now. And who knows? Maybe the robots will have already taken over by then.
